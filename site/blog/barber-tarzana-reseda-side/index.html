For Men in Tarzana and Reseda: Worth the Ten Minutes

5 min read The Amir Precision Team

A barber near Tarzana is usually a barber in Tarzana. This one is ten minutes east, on Ventura Boulevard in Encino, and the reason men make that drive is time rather than distance: an appointment that starts when it says it does, and finishes without being hurried into the next one.

Ten minutes, and what they cost

From central Tarzana it is about ten minutes east along Ventura Boulevard. From the Reseda side, coming down Hayvenhurst Avenue or Louise Avenue, closer to twelve. From Lake Balboa, allow fifteen. None of that is a long way by Valley standards — it is the sort of trip most men already make for a supermarket they prefer.

The part worth planning is the hour, not the mileage. Ventura Boulevard eastbound is clear before eleven and after seven, and slow from about four. A 9am appointment and a 7pm appointment both get you a fast run in each direction; a 5pm one taxes you twice. Tuesday through Friday the shop is open until 8, which makes the late slot a real choice rather than a theoretical one, and Saturday closes at 7.

If you are coming from the Reseda area or from anywhere near the Sepulveda Basin Recreation Area, the practical move is to attach the appointment to something you were already going to do on this stretch. Encino Park and Los Encinos State Historic Park are minutes away. Men who fold the cut into a run of errands stop thinking about the drive at all. If you are looking for a barber near Lake Balboa or the western end of the Basin, that is the framing that makes the distance disappear: it is not a separate journey, it is one more stop on a road you were already on.

Why the trip is not wasted

Because there is nothing to wait for when you arrive.

The shop takes appointments only. One barber, one chair. That is a limitation if you are two blocks away and want a cut in twenty minutes, and it is the entire point if you have just driven in from Tarzana — nobody who has spent twelve minutes on the boulevard should then spend twenty-five in a chair by the window watching two men get seen first.

It also means the slot is the slot. A shop that runs a queue absorbs its overruns by taking them out of whoever is next. A shop with one chair and a booked diary absorbs them by finishing properly and starting the following appointment a few minutes late, which is a smaller and more honest debt.

"If someone has driven in from Reseda I owe them the whole appointment, not the part that fits before the next man is due." — Amir, barber

Call or message on Instagram a day or two ahead midweek. Saturdays go earlier in the week, and that is the one day where turning up hopeful genuinely does not work.

Make the trip earn its keep

The service most men who drive in choose is the longer one, and it is a sensible instinct.

A haircut on its own is $50 and about forty-five minutes. A scissor cut and beard trim is $90 and an hour and a half — scissor work over the whole head for length and movement, with the beard shaped to sit against it. The second one is not a better version of the first; it is different work, and it is the one that changes how a head looks for a month rather than a fortnight.

If you are driving fifteen minutes each way, the arithmetic favours the longer appointment. Two thirty-minute visits cost you an hour of Ventura Boulevard between them. One ninety-minute visit costs you the same half hour of driving as a forty-five-minute one, and the result holds longer. Men from the Tarzana and Reseda side book the longer services more often than men who walk in from three blocks away, and that is not a coincidence.

A hot towel shave is its own appointment at $60. It is worth booking as a separate visit rather than tacking it on, because the pace is the product and there is no version of it that is quicker.

One thing not to do is book the longest service on your first visit. A first appointment is partly a conversation about what your hair actually does, and it is easier to have that conversation over a cut than over ninety minutes of scissor work committed to in advance. Come once for the haircut, see whether the shape holds three weeks later, and book the longer service after that. Driving in twice to find out is a smaller cost than driving in once and paying ninety for a shape you did not want.

Before you set off

Two things save the trip.

First, come with your hair the way it normally is. Not freshly wet, not flattened under a cap for the whole drive. How it sits when you walk in is the information the cut gets built on, and a cap for fifteen minutes on the 101 hides the crown entirely.

Second, know when you last had it cut, roughly. Three weeks and six weeks are different problems, and the answer changes what can be done in one sitting. If you are not sure, say so — a guess presented as a fact is worse than not knowing.

Directions and hours are on the site, along with the parking notes. Street parking runs along Ventura Boulevard and is metered on the blocks nearest the shop. Midweek it is easy. The common questions cover the rest.

FAQ

Frequently asked

How far is this barber from Tarzana?

About ten minutes east along Ventura Boulevard from central Tarzana, and roughly twelve from the Reseda side. Allow fifteen from Lake Balboa.

Can I get a cut in the Reseda area without an appointment here?

Not here — the shop is appointment-only, which is what makes a drive in from outside Encino worth making. A day or two ahead is usually enough midweek.

When is the easiest time of day to drive over?

Before eleven or after seven. Ventura Boulevard slows from about four, and the shop is open until 8 Tuesday to Friday, so an evening slot often means a faster trip both ways.

Is a longer appointment worth booking if I am driving in?

Usually. The driving costs the same whether the appointment is forty-five minutes or ninety, and the longer services hold their shape for longer, so the trip comes round less often.
