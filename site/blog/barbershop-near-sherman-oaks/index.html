Coming From Sherman Oaks for a Haircut

5 min read The Amir Precision Team

A barbershop near Sherman Oaks does not have to be in Sherman Oaks. Amir Precision Cuts is a short hop west along Ventura Boulevard, at 17388 in Encino. For most men on the Sherman Oaks side the drive is under ten minutes, and it buys a single-barber chair instead of whoever is free.

The trip west

Ventura Boulevard is the whole route. It runs continuously from Sherman Oaks into Encino, so there is no clever way to do it and no reason to look for one. You stay on the boulevard, you pass Hayvenhurst Avenue, and the shop is between there and Louise Avenue.

Traffic is the only variable worth planning around, and it behaves predictably. Late morning and early afternoon are clear. The stretch tightens up between about four and six in the evening, which is the window men most want, so a 7pm slot on a weekday is often a faster trip than a 5pm one even though it feels later. Tuesday to Friday the shop is open until 8, which makes that a real option rather than a theoretical one.

Coming from the eastern end of Sherman Oaks, near the 405, allow fifteen minutes at rush hour and eight otherwise. From the middle of Sherman Oaks it is less. Nobody is asking you to cross the Valley. This is one neighbourhood over. In practice that makes it an Encino barber for Sherman Oaks men as much as for Encino ones.

What changes at a shop with one barber

Most men in Sherman Oaks already have a haircut option closer than this one. Usually several. The question is not whether a closer chair exists — it is what the closer chair does differently, and the honest answer has nothing to do with skill and everything to do with structure.

At a shop with a rota, you book a haircut. You do not book a person. Whoever is free takes you, and the man who cut your hair well in March is not necessarily the man who cuts it in July. That means the growth pattern conversation resets every visit. Your cowlick gets rediscovered. The neckline decision gets made again, sometimes differently, and a neckline that has been blocked once and tapered the next time never quite settles.

Here there is one barber. Amir cuts every appointment, and the chair is not double-booked, which changes two things. The first is continuity: the second visit starts where the first one ended rather than at the beginning. The second is pace — nobody is being started in your slot, so the last five minutes of the cut are not being borrowed from someone else's first five.

"The second haircut is where a client finds out whether it was luck. If it is the same hands, it is not luck." — Amir, barber

None of that is an argument against the shop down the street from you. It is a description of a trade: a few more minutes in the car for a chair that remembers what your hair does.

What the appointment looks like

This barbershop near Sherman Oaks runs appointment-only, so the drive is never wasted on a queue. That is the main practical point for anyone travelling in from the Sherman Oaks side — you are not gambling twelve minutes of Ventura Boulevard on whether there is a seat.

A haircut runs about forty-five minutes. A haircut and beard trim together is $70 and takes about an hour and a quarter, and it is the appointment most men who drive in choose, because the two lines get shaped to work with each other in one visit rather than in two. If you are making a trip out of it anyway, doing both is the version that respects the trip.

Hours are Tuesday to Friday, 9 until 8, and Saturday 9 until 7. Closed Sunday and Monday. Saturday fills before the weekdays do, so if you want a Saturday morning slot, ask earlier in the week. Directions and hours are on the site, along with parking notes.

Speaking of which: street parking along Ventura Boulevard, metered on the closest blocks. Midweek it is easy. Saturday takes a few extra minutes. If you are driving in from Sherman Oaks on a Saturday, the parking is more likely to cost you time than the drive is.

Whether it is worth the ten minutes

That depends on something specific: how your last three haircuts compared to each other.

If they were the same cut three times, whatever you are doing is working, and there is no argument here worth reading. If they were three variations — one good, one flat, one where the back was suddenly higher than you wanted — then the inconsistency is structural rather than bad luck, and driving ten minutes west along the boulevard is a cheap way to fix a structural problem.

Men from Sherman Oaks, Van Nuys and Studio City come in for that reason more than any other. Not because Encino has better barbers — there is no argument being made about neighbourhoods here. Because one chair produces one result, and three visits to one chair produce three versions of the same haircut rather than three different haircuts. Consistency is an unglamorous thing to drive ten minutes for, and it is also the only thing most men actually want from a barber once they have found a shape that suits them.

The rest of the practical detail is in the common questions.

FAQ

Frequently asked

How far is this barbershop from Sherman Oaks?

Under ten minutes for most of Sherman Oaks, straight west along Ventura Boulevard. Allow fifteen at rush hour if you are coming from near the 405.

Do I need an appointment, or can I turn up?

An appointment. There is one barber and one chair, so there is no walk-in queue — which also means a drive in from Sherman Oaks is never wasted on a wait.

Will I get the same barber every visit?

Yes. Amir cuts every appointment, so the second haircut starts from what the first one established rather than from scratch.

When is the easiest time to drive over on a weekday?

Later rather than earlier. Ventura Boulevard tightens between about four and six, and the shop is open until 8 Tuesday through Friday, so a 7pm slot is often a quicker trip than a 5pm one.
